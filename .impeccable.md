## Design Context

### Users

This is a personal, glanceable home display for someone deciding when to leave for Manhattan from Nostrand Av. Its primary job is to make the next Manhattan-bound A/C arrival times readable from across a room; current local weather is the secondary job.

### Brand Personality

Direct, utilitarian, and recognizably New York. The interface should feel like purposeful MTA wayfinding rather than a generic smart-home dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use bold black-and-white transit signage adapted to the constraints of a 792×272 e-paper panel. Favor strong rules, compact all-caps labels, route circles, large tabular-feeling countdowns, and simple code-drawn weather symbols. Avoid decorative cards, destination details, soft consumer-dashboard styling, and visual clutter.

### Design Principles

- Make route and minutes legible within two seconds and from several feet away; use a star for the A express and a circle for the C local without repeating route names in text. Keep both supported countdown compositions: a left-aligned column for routine use and an optional relative-time layout that spreads arrivals horizontally by their delay from the soonest train.
- Show only information that changes the leave-now decision.
- Reserve roughly two-thirds of the screen for trains and one-third for weather.
- Use a 4-pixel spacing rhythm and strong section boundaries.
- Design for monochrome e-paper: bold silhouettes, no fine shading, and conservative refresh behavior.
